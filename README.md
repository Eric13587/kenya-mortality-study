# Actuarial Analysis of Mortality Trends in Kenya

**Eric Ndabi Mungai · Daystar University · Actuarial Science thesis**

## Research question

How did mortality among working-age adults in Kenya change from 2000 to 2021, and which age groups were most sensitive to those changes?

## Approach

I fitted separate Lee–Carter models for males and females aged 25–65 using Kenyan population mortality estimates from the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME). The model separates baseline mortality by age, each age group's sensitivity to change, and a time index that captures changes across calendar years. I used residual and stability diagnostics to assess the fitted trends.

## Findings

- The thesis reports average annual mortality improvements of **0.6% for males** and **1.0% for females** over the study period.
- The fitted model was most sensitive to movements in the mortality index at ages **37–42 for males** and **27–37 for females**.

The age-sensitivity parameter describes how strongly each age group responds to a change in the fitted index. It helps explain why mortality can change at different speeds across age groups even when they share an overall trend.

## Interpretation

The analysis uses modeled population estimates and covers working ages. Some diagnostics showed departures from the model assumptions. Applying these results to insurance pricing would require validating mortality assumptions against the experience of the insured population.
